We started with a glass of water.

ELMA began as a morning powder — hydration, beauty-from-within, clean energy, one glass before the day got loud. The skin care line is the same argument, made on the outside.

Why we extended the line

Hydration from the inside only gets you so far.

Our customers kept asking the same question: the powder handles the inside, so what should go on top of it? For two years we sent them to other brands. Most of what we found was fragranced, under-dosed, or wrapped in a twelve-step routine nobody keeps past February.

So we formulated it ourselves, with the same three rules the powder was built on: tell people the dose, leave out anything that is only there to make it feel expensive, and keep the routine short enough that it survives a bad week.

Seven products. Cleanse, hydrate, seal, protect — and two treatments for when the basics are steady. That is the whole line, and we are not in a rush to make it bigger.

How we formulate

Four rules we do not break, even when it costs us a launch.

i.

The barrier comes first

Every formula is pH-appropriate and built on ceramides, humectants, and emollients before a single active goes in. An active on a broken barrier is not a treatment — it is an irritant.

ii.

Percentages, on the label

Niacinamide 4%. Retinal 0.05%. THD ascorbate 10%. If we cannot print the number, the formula does not ship. No proprietary blends, no fairy dusting an ingredient for the marketing copy.

iii.

Fragrance-free, all of it

Fragrance is the leading cause of contact irritation in cosmetics and contributes nothing to skin function. Not essential oils either — "natural" is not the same as "non-irritating."

What we leave out

The list of things we will not put in a bottle is as considered as the list we will.

No synthetic fragrance or essential oils. No drying alcohols. No sulfates in the cleansers. No claims we could not defend to a dermatologist reading the ingredient list.
